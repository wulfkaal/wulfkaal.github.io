# kaal:position:2026-10-13-003

**Affirmed position.** The protected claim holds that economic institutions relying on lagging indicators, price signals, employment data and GDP reports, cannot detect AI driven transformation, because the transformation propagates faster than the monitoring systems built to observe it. Kaal's position applies that to intelligence proliferation: open weights, local inference and autonomous agents spread capability faster than the institutions that would register the change. The essay's further thesis, that abundance redistributes capability while authority stays concentrated with the few firms and states commanding frontier compute, is Kaal's own argument and not a consequence of the protected claim. The printing press is offered as a precedent for the lag, with presses licensed, censored and captured for generations until institutions caught up.

**Holds when.**

- The position applies the claim's detection failure to the spread of capability. The essay's separate thesis about where authority sits does not rest on the claim.
- The protected claim concerns detection failure in economic institutions relying on price signals, employment data and GDP reports. It does not establish how authority is distributed.
- The printing press comparison is offered as a precedent for institutional lag, not as a prediction of identical duration.
