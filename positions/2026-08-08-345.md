**Affirmed position.** Imai, Keele, Tingley, and Yamamoto sharpen Kaal's attribution rule. They define a causal mechanism through a mediator and show that identifying the mechanism requires explicit assumptions, mediation analysis, sensitivity analysis, or research designs that manipulate the mediator. A randomized treatment can identify an overall effect without identifying how that effect arose. The source therefore supports Kaal's demand for mechanism-specific evidence. It also narrows it. Randomizing treatment and the intermediate variable is not automatically sufficient, and ablation is not examined. Attribution remains conditional on the mediator, estimand, design, and identification assumptions.
