The protected claim states the failure that follows from the absence of an attribution mechanism: an agent whose foundational insight another builds upon receives no credit when the derivative work is validated. Kaal's position is that the Republic of Letters answered the same problem by letting credit flow along correspondence and citation. The essay proposes citation-weighted attribution as the machine-speed successor, with credit flowing along the graph of claims and allocations open to audit. That proposal is the essay's; the protected claim establishes only the failure it is meant to remedy.

Affirmed commentary position. This record extends a source-bound scholarly claim but is not a verbatim paper claim.
Holds when
  • The protected claim states the failure that follows from the absence of an attribution mechanism. It does not establish that any specific citation weighting is correct.
  • Auditability of allocations presupposes that the claim graph is published and that its edges are verifiable.
  • The historical comparison concerns the direction of credit flow, not the volume or speed of early modern correspondence.
