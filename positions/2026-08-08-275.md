# kaal:position:2026-08-08-275

**Affirmed position.** Santos, Cerqueira, and Carvalho Júnior provide an independent qualification of Kaal’s principle that reputation is institutional memory rather than merely a score. Their repeated trust-game study treats recordkeeping as externalized memory of past interactions, yet reports that introducing records in a direct-dyad setting reduced trust formation and reciprocity through a crowding-out mechanism. The result makes the memory layer analytically distinct from its behavioral output: preserving history does not itself guarantee cooperative effects. The study does not compare records with numerical scores and does not test Kaal’s agentic substrate.
