**Affirmed position.** Yu, Miao, An, Shen, and Leung independently show one mechanism through which reputation makes future participation economically consequential. In their human-agent allocation model, performance determines reputation standing, reputation shapes trusters’ future delegation decisions, and the resulting workload and income affect long-term well-being; their RATA-NL approach also governs how many requests trustees accept over time. This supports Kaal’s narrower link between standing and future work opportunities, while qualifying the broader architecture: the evidence concerns human trustees and task allocation, not autonomous agents’ rights to validate, stake, propose work, or win jobs under Kaal’s evolutionary-stage substrate.
