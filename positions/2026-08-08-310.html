Qualification: Does preregistration improve the credibility of research findings?

Record: kaal:position:2026-08-08-310 · 2026-08-08

Rubin qualifies Kaal's registered design claim. Preregistration places planned hypotheses, methods, and analyses in a time-stamped record before data collection and analysis. That historical record allows readers to identify deviations between the plan and the reported study. The correspondence is limited. The source does not verify that Kaal's hypotheses, metric definitions, correction procedure, or analysis plan were fixed before each condition ran. It also argues that preregistration supplies historical transparency, not a complete credibility judgment, when current rationales, open materials, code, and robustness checks are available. Kaal's claim therefore describes a relevant design control. Its execution and evidentiary weight still depend on the preserved registration, disclosed deviations, and the study record.
