# kaal:position:2026-08-08-317

**Affirmed position.** Niemeyer, Veri, Dryzek, and Bächtiger qualify Kaal's prediction about deliberation and unanimity. Their account does not treat opinion change, disagreement, or raw agreement as sufficient evidence of deliberative quality. Opinion change may arise from polarization, while high agreement may still fail to express deliberative reason. The authors instead ask whether a group has integrated relevant considerations within a shared representational framework. Agreement and disagreement matter only when judgments remain coherent with that framework.

The distinction gives Kaal's prediction a limited institutional meaning. A reduction in unanimity may be relevant when deliberation exposes considerations that earlier votes failed to integrate, but the resulting divergence is not itself the objective. It indicates a representational update only when participants respond to relevant reasons and their judgments become intersubjectively consistent. The source also cautions against treating consensus as the ideal. Its preferred outcome is higher-level agreement about which considerations matter and how they bear on action.

The scope is narrower than Kaal's study. Niemeyer and coauthors examine human deliberative forums, not LLM validators, and they measure deliberative reason rather than unanimity. They do not test Kaal's treatment or directional hypothesis. Their work supports only the rationale for separating deliberative quality from the amount of agreement. Kaal's empirical prediction remains distinct.
