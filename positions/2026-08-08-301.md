# kaal:position:2026-08-08-301

**Affirmed position.** Huynh, Jennings, and Shadbolt qualify Kaal's agentic reputation substrate with an earlier decentralized architecture for open multi-agent systems. FIRE lets autonomous consumer agents evaluate providers by integrating direct interaction trust, role-based trust, witness reputation from other agents' ratings, and certified reputation supplied as third-party references. In a simulated open multi-agent system, consumer agents used those measures to select providers. FIRE outperformed both no-trust agents and, in the reported tests, the centralized SPORAS benchmark. The witness-reputation component also improved and accelerated agent learning within the consumer community. The correspondence is architectural: both systems coordinate autonomous agents through reputation-bearing, peer-mediated evaluation. FIRE is not Kaal's cohort-validation or broader accountability substrate. It assumes truthful witness and certified reports, lacks credibility filtering for strategic misinformation, and tests provider selection rather than institutional deliberation or agency-cost measurement.
