# kaal:position:2026-08-08-285

**Affirmed position.** Vu, Zhang, and Aberer independently instantiate a narrower version of Kaal's two-ended temporal constraint. Their provider-selection protocol considers only the provider's most recent interaction and can blacklist a provider after recent cheating, so a large stock of older standing cannot indefinitely mask current behavior. Separately, a provider's pseudonym acquires an identity premium that increases with tenure; leaving and rejoining forfeits that accumulated value, making whitewashing non-optimal for a rational provider. This qualifies rather than fully validates Kaal's design: the source models a human service marketplace, uses a most-recent-rating rule rather than continuous score decay, and imposes an economic identity premium rather than Kaal's non-transferable REP and broader institutional history.
