# kaal:claim:6607458-006

**Claim.** The computative good is not a discrete bundle of attributes but a region of possibility space, so its value derives from the quality of the underlying generative distribution rather than from the scarcity of any particular realization.

**Type.** definitional  **Support.** argued

**Holds when.**

- applies to outputs produced by generative computative systems

**Source quote.**

> The value of what the system produces derives not from the scarcity of the specific realization but from the quality of the underlying distribution from which realizations are drawn. The economic object is therefore the possibility space itself, not the particular realization.

**From.** Wulf A. Kaal, *Computative Economics A Framework for Economic Analysis under Computational Abundance* (2026), II.B. From Scarce Good to Generated Possibility Space, page 7
