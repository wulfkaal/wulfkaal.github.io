  1. How the agent, harness and model components are identified separately from outcomes alone remains an open question. 2026
  2. A record keyed to model weights merges what should be separate and a record keyed only to context splits what should merge, so grounding is the identity rule and the rule is a design choice and not a fact to be discovered. 2026
  3. The identity rule has three nameable error rates, and capacity depends on them in closed form. 2026
  4. The model assumes the obligation attaches to the record and does not follow the new identity, which encodes the proposition that identity is the persistent record. 2026
  5. A record can fail to answer for a large harm in two different ways: where entry is costly the injured party receives all of the agent's garnishable earnings, and where entry is cheap the injured party receives nothing because the agent leaves. 2026
  6. Partial linkage across identities raises capacity and is the operative parameter of any registry that tries to make a new identity inherit its predecessor's record. 2026
  7. Where every level of a sponsorship chain has costly entry, recovery degrades gracefully and the chain recovers the lesser of the harm and the sum of the levels' capacities, so capacities add. 2026
  8. In a chain of cliff agents capacities do not add and the chain is only as good as its best single member. 2026
  9. Adding sponsors adds nothing when exit is cheap, because each agent in turn leaves when the obligation reaches it, and a sponsor's guarantee is worth the sum of its parts only where exit is costly. 2026
  10. Accountable agents can sponsor other agents only within limits: under a shared fault a sponsor's capacity is eaten by the same slash each sub-agent's harm imposes on it, and the principal's exposure returns at the sponsor level. 2026
  11. Registries should treat linkage across identities as the principal design parameter. 2026
  12. The record can become the target of optimization, which degrades it as a metric, and the Article reports that this effect is untested. 2026
  13. If an existing standard or off-chain layer already supplies a verified, non-transferable, adverse-retaining history, the Article's contribution narrows to the attribution of agent, harness and model. 2026
  14. The verification record can be expressed as a W3C Verifiable Credential whose issuer is the verifier pool, whose subject is the agent identifier and whose claims are adjudicated task counts, agreement rate with ground truth, over-approval rate, slash events and the open obligation, with a validity window. 2026
  15. Neither the Verifiable Credential nor the ERC-8004 feedback format has a field for an obligation, a slash or a stake, so the consequence side of the record cannot be expressed in either. 2026
  16. The ERC-8004 Identity Registry token is a transferable ERC-721, so the identity layer places no exit cost on a holder. 2026
  17. Of the ERC-8004 feedback events in the window, 91.2 percent carry the tag starred and 90.3 percent carry the value 100, while only 9.2 percent carry a feedback URI, 6.6 percent a nonzero hash and 1.6 percent an endpoint. 2026
  18. In the window 97.6 percent of ERC-8004 clients rated exactly one agent, for the median agent the top client supplies all of its feedback, and only two agents have five or more feedback events. 2026
  19. In the window there were 5,496 post-mint transfers of ERC-8004 registry tokens and 5,488 of the window's own registrations were later transferred to an address other than the registered owner, a pattern the logs cannot distinguish from sales. 2026
  20. On the ERC-8004 registry in the studied window nearly all feedback is a single unverifiable five-star-style signal from a one-time client, with almost no evidence attached and no consequence semantics. 2026
  21. Current agent registries do not carry the consequence side of the record, and the existing standards have no field for an obligation, a slash or a stake. 2026
