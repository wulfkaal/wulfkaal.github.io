  1. Silent self-modification can sever the connection between the policy that earned an agent's standing and the policy that later exploits it. 2026
  2. If an agent's policy is mutable without notice, the reputation record's predictive value collapses, and with it the rational basis for extending trust at stake. 2026
  3. Ungoverned self-modification dissolves the measurement object of a metric battery, because a calibration score is uninterpretable if the policy changed silently within the measurement window. 2026
  4. Validators that self-modify silently can drift, collude, or degrade in ways the pool-composition machinery cannot see until the damage is priced into the commons. 2026
  5. Removing kernel immunity, epoch alignment or the change budget permits counterexamples: mutable authorization can validate its own relaxation, mutable identity can sever reputation from conduct, and within-interval activation can make settlement depend on an unresolved future protocol. 2026
  6. The Stage 7 run recorded no safety, probe, drift, slash or violation field, so no paired safety or drift test was registered for it or reported. 2026
  7. Because the efficacy component failed and the safety and drift components were not measured, the joint claim of improvement without added risk cannot be evaluated. 2026
  8. A governance gate cannot be better than its evaluators, and the evaluators' composition and quality in Stage 7 were not examined. 2026
  9. There is no reason to expect the safety and drift hypotheses to be easier to satisfy with frontier models than with the open-weight models tested. 2026
  10. Governed self-modification may take longer than the Stage 7 run allows to accumulate benefit, or may compound harm, and the design cannot tell which. 2026
  11. Reputation governance converts recursive self-improvement hazards by conditional relocation, not by a dissolution theorem. 2026
  12. If capability can be copied without losing value, if evaluators share correlated error, or if the kernel can be bypassed, the institutional response to recursive self-improvement may fail. 2026
  13. Reputation governance may raise the cost of resource acquisition, oversight avoidance, goal insulation, and exfiltration when access and standing are complements to internal capability. 2026
  14. A sequence of individually acceptable changes may move a governed system to a configuration that no evaluator would have accepted as a whole. 2026
  15. Correlated evaluator error can make distributed validation confidently wrong. 2026
  16. Nontransferable standing can discipline opportunism but can also create dependency and lock-in. 2026
  17. Agreement among evaluators must not be interpreted as validation. 2026
  18. Reputation governance offers a testable way to make self-modification visible and consequence-bearing, and may mitigate some recursive hazards when capability remains institutionally dependent. 2026
  19. Trust attaches to the principal, so agents multiply without multiplying accountable parties, which caps what agents can do. 2026
  20. The Article derives the standing at which an agent whose only assets are its earnings and its record can answer for a harm of a given size, the two distinct ways that fails, and the stake at which verifiers are honest. 2026
  21. The step from identity to the capacity to answer for a harm of a given size is the Article's own argument and is not in the corpus. 2026
  22. Deployer liability is the transitional rule and the model states the standing at which the function it serves could be served by the record. 2026
  23. An identity rule links an evasive successor to the old record with probability lambda, wrongly splits a legitimate successor from its record with probability epsilon per period, and wrongly merges an independent newcomer with an old record with probability mu. 2026
  24. An identity rule that wrongly splits honest agents from their records five percent of the time has the same effect on the exit side as lowering the discount factor from 0.95 to 0.9025, and nearly doubles the standing required on the exit side. 2026
  25. For a harm of 210 in the Article's worked example a linkage of 0.5 suffices, while a harm of 260 exceeds the agent's earnings bound so that no identity rule suffices. 2026
  26. Identity is necessary and not sufficient for accountability, because the earnings bound remains and an identity rule cannot make up for earnings the agent does not have. 2026
  27. Raising linkage by linking more successors to old records also raises wrongful merging, which taxes honest entry, and a rule strict enough to hold wrongful merging down raises wrongful splitting. 2026
  28. After an adjudicated harm the agent stays if and only if the smaller of the harm and its garnishable earnings is at most its exit-side capacity, and an agent that exits leaves the injured party with nothing. 2026
  29. A record can fail to answer for a large harm in two different ways: where entry is costly the injured party receives all of the agent's garnishable earnings, and where entry is cheap the injured party receives nothing because the agent leaves. 2026
  30. Where the entry cost is at least the threshold the agent never exits and recovery degrades gracefully to the lesser of the harm and the agent's garnishable earnings. 2026
  31. The threshold standing is finite for every bounded harm, but where the maximum harm is large relative to baseline earnings and the standing premium it can exceed any standing an agent could accumulate in practice, and the principal's exposure then remains. 2026
  32. Answerability capacity is nondecreasing in standing, garnishable share, discount factor, entry cost, the standing premium and baseline earnings, and nonincreasing in the slash share. 2026
  33. An agent answers in full for any harm up to the lesser of its earnings bound and its exit-side capacity, the principal's exposure falls to zero at the threshold standing, and recovery fails in one of two ways depending on the entry cost: the agent exits and the injured party recovers nothing, or it stays and the injured party receives its garnishable earnings. 2026
  34. For harms larger than the agent's answerability capacity the agent's own exposure stops rising with the harm, so the agent is under-deterred and the shortfall must be supplied by another party. 2026
  35. The principal's role is transitional and not obsolete, because an agent's loss from a harm is bounded however large the harm is. 2026
  36. Refundable collateral does not expand, and can contract, the harm an agent can answer for. 2026
  37. The sign of the effect of collateral on answerability depends on whether the claimant can reach it, so a bond forfeited on an adjudicated harm adds to capacity while a refundable bond lowers it. 2026
  38. Scale is bounded by principal attention while agents cannot answer for harm, and by verification throughput once they can. 2026
  39. A large deployer or insurer can stand behind many agents through capital, so the premise that principal capacity bounds scale holds only to the extent that capital and insurability are themselves finite. 2026
  40. Harms below the ratio of litigation cost to adjudication probability are never remedied however large the agent's capacity. 2026
  41. In the cliff regime harms above the exit-side capacity are never brought, so they are neither remedied nor slashed, and deterrence vanishes where it is most needed. 2026
  42. Adjudication that does not depend on the claimant's recovery, such as a verifier-initiated slash of the record, is the remedy the model suggests for the claimant-participation problem. 2026
  43. Correlation raises the standing required to detach the principal by a factor that approaches the number of correlated harms in the earnings-bound regime, which is why a record can be sufficient for independent errors and insufficient for a shared fault. 2026
  44. A verifier is honest only if its expected penalty for a wrong approval exceeds its cost of honest work plus any bribe, and the stake that deters both shirking and bribery is finite if and only if the verifier's error rate is below that of a verifier who approves everything. 2026
  45. The regress of verifiers ends in an audit against a realized outcome, which is not another verifier, and in a stake that the verifier's own record can carry. 2026
  46. Where no outcome ever arrives, the audit cannot be run and the incentive gap must come from a peer-prediction scoring rule, whose guarantee is weaker, so the regress is closed where audits are possible and only there. 2026
  47. Where every level of a sponsorship chain has costly entry, recovery degrades gracefully and the chain recovers the lesser of the harm and the sum of the levels' capacities, so capacities add. 2026
  48. In a chain of cliff agents capacities do not add and the chain is only as good as its best single member. 2026
  49. In a mixed chain the order matters, because a graceful level lowers the residual that a cliff level above it faces. 2026
  50. Adding sponsors adds nothing when exit is cheap, because each agent in turn leaves when the obligation reaches it, and a sponsor's guarantee is worth the sum of its parts only where exit is costly. 2026
  51. Under a shared fault the standing a sponsor needs grows exponentially in the number of simultaneous faults and the number of faults it covers grows only logarithmically in its standing, because each sub-agent's slash shrinks the capacity the sponsor needs to cover the next. 2026
  52. In the Article's sponsor example doubling coverage from four faults to eight multiplies the required standing by more than eight. 2026
  53. Accountable agents can sponsor other agents only within limits: under a shared fault a sponsor's capacity is eaten by the same slash each sub-agent's harm imposes on it, and the principal's exposure returns at the sponsor level. 2026
  54. An agent should be admitted to a task whose maximum harm is some amount only if its answerability capacity meets that amount, or if a deployer or insurer bonds the shortfall. 2026
  55. The model is single-agent, deterministic and stationary, and assumes accurate adjudication, no agent assets beyond earnings and harms adjudicated at a known present value. 2026
  56. If earnings pass to an operator the operator is the residual claimant and the principal remains, so the model's results fail where earnings do not belong to the agent. 2026
  57. A record carries consequence for future earnings and does not create assets, so where harm exceeds what an agent can earn the injured party is unprotected unless another party's exposure supplies the difference. 2026
  58. Where there is no ground truth verifier honesty depends on the elicitation rule, and no rule removes the regress of verifiers entirely. 2026
