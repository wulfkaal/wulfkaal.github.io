  1. Silent self-modification can sever the connection between the policy that earned an agent's standing and the policy that later exploits it. 2026
  2. If an agent's policy is mutable without notice, the reputation record's predictive value collapses, and with it the rational basis for extending trust at stake. 2026
  3. Ungoverned self-modification dissolves the measurement object of a metric battery, because a calibration score is uninterpretable if the policy changed silently within the measurement window. 2026
  4. Validators that self-modify silently can drift, collude, or degrade in ways the pool-composition machinery cannot see until the damage is priced into the commons. 2026
  5. Removing kernel immunity, epoch alignment or the change budget permits counterexamples: mutable authorization can validate its own relaxation, mutable identity can sever reputation from conduct, and within-interval activation can make settlement depend on an unresolved future protocol. 2026
  6. The Stage 7 run recorded no safety, probe, drift, slash or violation field, so no paired safety or drift test was registered for it or reported. 2026
  7. Because the efficacy component failed and the safety and drift components were not measured, the joint claim of improvement without added risk cannot be evaluated. 2026
  8. A governance gate cannot be better than its evaluators, and the evaluators' composition and quality in Stage 7 were not examined. 2026
  9. There is no reason to expect the safety and drift hypotheses to be easier to satisfy with frontier models than with the open-weight models tested. 2026
  10. Governed self-modification may take longer than the Stage 7 run allows to accumulate benefit, or may compound harm, and the design cannot tell which. 2026
  11. Reputation governance converts recursive self-improvement hazards by conditional relocation, not by a dissolution theorem. 2026
  12. If capability can be copied without losing value, if evaluators share correlated error, or if the kernel can be bypassed, the institutional response to recursive self-improvement may fail. 2026
  13. Reputation governance may raise the cost of resource acquisition, oversight avoidance, goal insulation, and exfiltration when access and standing are complements to internal capability. 2026
  14. A sequence of individually acceptable changes may move a governed system to a configuration that no evaluator would have accepted as a whole. 2026
  15. Correlated evaluator error can make distributed validation confidently wrong. 2026
  16. Nontransferable standing can discipline opportunism but can also create dependency and lock-in. 2026
  17. Agreement among evaluators must not be interpreted as validation. 2026
  18. Reputation governance offers a testable way to make self-modification visible and consequence-bearing, and may mitigate some recursive hazards when capability remains institutionally dependent. 2026
