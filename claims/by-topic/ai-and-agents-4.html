  1. In a mixed chain the order matters, because a graceful level lowers the residual that a cliff level above it faces. 2026
  2. Adding sponsors adds nothing when exit is cheap, because each agent in turn leaves when the obligation reaches it, and a sponsor's guarantee is worth the sum of its parts only where exit is costly. 2026
  3. Under a shared fault the standing a sponsor needs grows exponentially in the number of simultaneous faults and the number of faults it covers grows only logarithmically in its standing, because each sub-agent's slash shrinks the capacity the sponsor needs to cover the next. 2026
  4. In the Article's sponsor example doubling coverage from four faults to eight multiplies the required standing by more than eight. 2026
  5. Accountable agents can sponsor other agents only within limits: under a shared fault a sponsor's capacity is eaten by the same slash each sub-agent's harm imposes on it, and the principal's exposure returns at the sponsor level. 2026
  6. An agent should be admitted to a task whose maximum harm is some amount only if its answerability capacity meets that amount, or if a deployer or insurer bonds the shortfall. 2026
  7. Registries should treat linkage across identities as the principal design parameter. 2026
  8. The model is single-agent, deterministic and stationary, and assumes accurate adjudication, no agent assets beyond earnings and harms adjudicated at a known present value. 2026
  9. The model presupposes a rule that treats the registered record as a holder of earnings, which is the personhood question the Article does not resolve. 2026
  10. The model's parameters are not calibrated to any market, and its results are claims about what a design would have to satisfy and not about any legal system. 2026
  11. If earnings pass to an operator the operator is the residual claimant and the principal remains, so the model's results fail where earnings do not belong to the agent. 2026
  12. A record carries consequence for future earnings and does not create assets, so where harm exceeds what an agent can earn the injured party is unprotected unless another party's exposure supplies the difference. 2026
  13. Legal personhood raises its own risks, because Bryson, Diamantis and Grant identify abuse and incoherence in precedents that used fictions of personhood, and the model does not answer that concern as a matter of law. 2026
  14. Where there is no ground truth verifier honesty depends on the elicitation rule, and no rule removes the regress of verifiers entirely. 2026
  15. The record can become the target of optimization, which degrades it as a metric, and the Article reports that this effect is untested. 2026
  16. If an existing standard or off-chain layer already supplies a verified, non-transferable, adverse-retaining history, the Article's contribution narrows to the attribution of agent, harness and model. 2026
  17. The Article's two registered tests used a prompted language model with no stake and test whether a model applies a stated decision rule and not whether agents holding earnings respond to incentive. 2026
  18. The model generates six hypotheses with nulls, of which the first three were tested and the remaining three, on claimant participation, verifier stake and chain recovery, were untested. 2026
  19. The Article's registered studies are mechanism demonstrations with prompted agents from one provider and do not show that deployed agents respond to such obligations or bear on any legal question. 2026
  20. In Study 1 the registered prediction for the cell with obligation 230 and entry cost 60 failed because the benchmark equated full answerability with staying, while the agents stayed and paid what they could as the corrected rule predicts. 2026
  21. In Study 2, registered after the correction, all three registered hypotheses were rejected as nulls under Holm correction. 2026
  22. The step in honoring between obligations of 205 and 215 at entry cost 10 and its shift with entry cost are the location the corrected rule predicts, which a monotone heuristic does not locate. 2026
  23. The Article's novelty search ran 12 queries against OpenAlex and arXiv, returned 301 records and 251 unique records, and 33 addressed four or more of the components, with screening and reading done by the author's AI assistant and not by independent reviewers. 2026
  24. The novelty search found no record that states the full chain from verification at scale to identity to accountability to the principal's changed role, and the closest work treats agent identity, accountability and shortfall bonding separately. 2026
  25. The Article's claim of novelty is limited to the combination and to the explicit thresholds and remains open to refutation by a document the search did not find. 2026

The verification record can be expressed as a W3C Verifiable Credential whose issuer is the verifier pool, whose subject is the agent identifier and whose claims are adjudicated task counts, agreement rate with ground truth, over-approval rate, slash events and the open obligation, with a validity window. 2026
  27. Neither the Verifiable Credential nor the ERC-8004 feedback format has a field for an obligation, a slash or a stake, so the consequence side of the record cannot be expressed in either. 2026

  30. Current agent registries do not carry the consequence side of the record, and the existing standards have no field for an obligation, a slash or a stake. 2026
  31. The Article does not establish that any legal system will recognize agent accountability and does not say what the law should provide. 2026
