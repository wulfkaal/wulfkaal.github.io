458 atomic, individually citable claims from the published work of Wulf A. Kaal tagged ai-and-agents.

Pages: 1 · 2 · 3
  1. On the measured cohort, the highest-record selector beats uniform selection by 0.243 with a ninety-five percent interval of [0.102, 0.384]. 2026
  2. The measured selector is an argmax over a record, not the reputation-weighted aggregation specified by RLRF, and the cohort contains no validation pool, stake, or deliberation round. 2026
  3. The cohort does not show that the highest-record selector beats shipping an artifact that passes the visible checks because the measured margin is 0.037 and its interval contains zero. 2026
  4. The 225-configuration sweep covers 4,500 trials, treats party accuracy as an input, and is reported as simulation rather than validation. 2026
  5. No policy is trained anywhere in the paper. 2026
  6. The contribution beyond prior work is a proposal to treat alignment as a Computative Economics mechanism design problem, and none of the evidence establishes that this reading is correct. 2026
  7. The canonical RLHF pipeline consists of supervised fine-tuning, reward-model training on human preference comparisons, and policy optimization. 2026
  8. Reward-model overoptimization can raise proxy reward while degrading true reward as optimized policies move farther from the reference policy. 2026
  9. Inter-annotator agreement rates are compatible with an incentive account, but ambiguity, plural values, rater error, underspecified instructions, heterogeneity, and legitimate preference diversity can produce the same rates. 2026
  10. The Computative Economics framing replaces a preference holder choosing fixed alternatives with a generator-evaluator tuple whose activity produces the alternative set. 2026
  11. RLRF uses a two-round validation protocol whose validators stake non-transferable reputation and whose domain models update through verification. 2026
  12. Pairwise disagreement measures label instability but does not identify annotator error variance or a structural variance floor without an explicit latent-label model. 2026
  13. Arrow's theorem is only an analogy to RLHF aggregation and does not derive reward hacking or overoptimization. 2026
  14. The incomplete-contracts comparison treats out-of-distribution behavior as unresolved, but remains an analogy rather than a derivation of reward overoptimization. 2026
  15. RLAIF addresses scalability by substituting AI-generated preferences, but it propagates the AI labeler's own biases through the pipeline. 2026
  16. Reliability-weighted assessment is established prior work, while RLRF's stated difference is that the weight is an economic position the assessor can lose. 2026
  17. RLAIF and Constitutional AI remove the human scalability bottleneck, but neither gives the model labeler anything to lose. 2026
  18. Verifiable-reward reinforcement learning already supplies deterministic rewards where answer keys exist, while the claim that a staked pool can substitute where no key exists is not established here. 2026
  19. Format D combines reasoning supervision with adversarial argument, and its claimed increment is selecting revisions by which arguments moved a staked assessor, an increment no experiment in the paper tests. 2026
  20. The paper's remaining residue is a combination rather than a primitive, and it is not compared with the nearest sequential peer-prediction, prediction-market, staking, or debate mechanisms. 2026
  21. For each prompt and candidate output, RLRF selects a validation pool by verifiable random function from agents holding relevant expertise tags. 2026
  22. RLRF permits validators to have different objective functions and domain models, treating honest disagreement on ambiguous cases as epistemic signal and systematic deviation as a possible adversarial signal. 2026
  23. The multiplicative-weights regret bound limits performance against the realized pool resolution, but does not show that the pool resolution is externally correct. 2026
  24. It remains unproved whether equilibrium reputation weights track externally checked accuracy rather than pool agreement, or whether keyed-task weights transfer to unkeyed tasks. 2026
  25. Validators face consequences for disagreement with an unkeyed pool or error against a keyed result, but this only creates an incentive to match the recorded resolution and does not prove an unkeyed resolution correct. 2026
  26. The two-round protocol is proposed to collect more structured data than a scalar label, but the paper does not establish that the added fields contain more information about policy quality. 2026
  27. Round 1 is a zero-slashing cheap-talk round that elicits private information without economic commitment. 2026
  28. The proposed debate transport layer is not built or exercised, and none of the paper's evidence includes a debate corpus. 2026
  29. The vote-change delta is a deterministic function of the two vote rounds and therefore adds no separate information channel. 2026
  30. The vote-change delta is constructional rather than informational because it identifies which events produce a Format D example and how to construct that example. 2026
  31. Format D would train on arguments accompanying a staked vote change, but that use is untested. 2026
  32. Equation 5 is a mutual-information identity rather than a Fisher-information decomposition, so the Fisher question does not follow from it. 2026
  33. Every mutual-information term in equation 5 is nonnegative, but the paper shows none to be strictly positive. 2026
  34. The data processing inequality cannot compare RLHF and RLRF because their labels arise from different observation mechanisms and no processing chain connects them. 2026
  35. The two rounds are intended to capture contested quality dimensions and the reasons they matter, but the paper does not show that either collection is informative about policy quality. 2026
  36. Format D complements three additional training formats to produce a four-format instruction-tuning taxonomy. 2026
  37. The proposed four-format flywheel is not measured or implemented and is listed as an open problem. 2026
  38. The paper withdrew its earlier Cramer-Rao efficiency conjecture because revisions could not make it coherent and comparable with deployed RLHF, and it makes no mean squared error claim. 2026
  39. The Fisher-information comparison lacks a common observation model, and rationality plus the stated non-babbling and positive-information conditions do not establish a strict ordering. 2026
  40. Equilibrium-trajectory separation cannot be evaluated until a common stochastic process, its filtrations, a concentration functional, and a cross-mode estimand are defined. 2026
  41. The paper withdraws its comparison between a 49 percent simulated Sybil share and classical Byzantine fault tolerance because the two limits are different. 2026
  42. A reward signal based only on an externally checked outcome can be style-invariant by construction, but this does not extend to a pool resolution or fitted reward model. 2026
  43. Remark 1 states only signal invariance and supports no policy-level consequence without a separability model and empirical training evidence. 2026
  44. The measured cohort contains real artifacts and deterministic verdicts, but contains no validation pool, stake, deliberation round, or reputation weight from the proposed mechanism. 2026
  45. Across all 564 records, checked-case and withheld-case accuracy have a rank correlation of +0.811, with mean accuracies of 0.702 and 0.608. 2026
  46. Fifty-three cohort records passed every visible check and failed withheld checks, but adversarial configurations were instructed to produce this behavior, so the number is not a base rate. 2026
  47. The record's measured advantage over passing visible checks changes sign under an arbitrary file-order tie-break, so the quantity is not resolved at this sample size. 2026
  48. The sweep's 67.7 percent annotator baseline is a simulated output rather than a published agreement rate or a measurement of human annotators. 2026
  49. The sweep's reported RLRF accuracy above 0.97 is an output of parameters set by the sweep, not a measurement of a system. 2026
  50. Finding 4 supports no tuning conclusion because the paper reports no parameter ranking or supporting values for it. 2026
  51. Finding 7 reports opposing stability orderings but does not define or test equilibrium-trajectory separation. 2026
  52. The three-stage RLRF training pipeline is a proposal, and the paper does not establish a signal-quality advantage before specifying it. 2026
  53. A fitted reputation reward model can learn style correlations absent from an externally checked outcome, and no such reward model is trained or tested in the paper. 2026
  54. RLRF is intended to make governance adapt through evolving reputation weights, but whether those weights track validator accuracy or transfer between keyed and unkeyed tasks remains unproved. 2026
  55. The paper does not establish that the RLRF best-response map satisfies the continuity, convexity, or contraction conditions required for equilibrium and convergence. 2026
  56. The first research requirement is an end-to-end training comparison that separately ablates reputation weighting, the two-round split, debate, and Format D. 2026
  57. Experiment T did not show that consensus-weighted selection outperformed equal weighting because the +0.0017 difference had a ninety-five percent interval from -0.0051 to +0.0085. 2026
  58. Recovering previously unparsed assessor replies reversed Experiment T's magnitude estimate to -0.0017 while leaving its interval inclusive of zero and its attribution result below the required percentile. 2026
