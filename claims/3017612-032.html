Because shareholder agreements are designed to displace corporate formalities such as majority rule, it is reasonable to require that the intended degree of deviation be set out explicitly in the agreement.

When a shareholder agreement is included in the corporate charter or bylaws, it becomes subject to amendment as provided therein or by statute.
