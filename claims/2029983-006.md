# kaal:claim:2029983-006

**Claim.** Jurisdictional competition adapts legal rules to changed economic circumstances faster than harmonization does, because a single jurisdiction can change its rule unilaterally whereas harmonized regimes require all jurisdictions to agree on a rule before it can change.

**Type.** mechanism  **Support.** argued

**Holds when.**

- underlying market conditions are changing
- actors have imperfect information about optimal rules

**Source quote.**

> When underlying economic circumstances change, it is more likely that a jurisdiction will change its rules if there is juris- dictional competition than if all jurisdictions are bound to agree upon the same rule before they change it.

**From.** Wulf A. Kaal, Richard W. Painter, *Forum Competition and Choice of Law Competition in Securities Law after Morrison v. National Austral* (2012), I. The Fundamentals of Jurisdictional Competition, page 15
