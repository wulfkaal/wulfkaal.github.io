# kaal:claim:1765901-016

**Claim.** Treating the U.S. location of a broker as making the securities purchase domestic would circumvent most of the Morrison holding, since a foreign buyer of foreign securities could invoke U.S. law simply by routing the order through a U.S. broker.

**Type.** failure  **Support.** argued

**Holds when.**

- orders placed through U.S. brokers for execution on foreign exchanges

**Source quote.**

> The U.S. broker places the order based on an order from a foreign client, and U.S. securities law arguably would apply to the securities purchase, even if executed on a non-US. exchange. This line of argument would circum- vent most of the holding in Morrison.

**From.** Kaal and Painter, *The Aftermath of Morrison v. National Australia Bank and Elliott Associates v. Porsche* (2011), II. Open Questions After Morrison; 4. Does a Transaction Take Place in the U.S. if a U.S. Broker Is Involved?, page 15
