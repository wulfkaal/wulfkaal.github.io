- [2834531-022](https://wulfkaal.github.io/claims/2834531-022) [failure/argued] *(failure mode)* -- Time pressure produces two distinct fact failures: the facts about a new technology may simply not exist yet, or regulators may select the wrong, contested, or irrelevant facts as the basis of regulation.
  > The pressure of time means that the facts surrounding a piece of new technology or other innovation may not be there, or the regulators may simply select the "wrong" — or at least contested or otherwise irrelevant — facts as the basis of regulation.
  Mark Fenwick, Wulf A. Kaal, Erik P. M. Vermeulen, Regulation Tomorrow What Happens When Technology Is Faster Than the Law (2017). SSRN: https://ssrn.com/abstract=2834531
- [2834531-023](https://wulfkaal.github.io/claims/2834531-023) [mechanism/argued] *(failure mode)* -- In the Airbnb case, the selection of the relevant facts and the residency rule that followed from it benefit vested interests, most obviously the hotel industry that stands to lose from Airbnb's competition.
  > Of course, the selection of the "relevant facts" in this case and the resulting rule benefits certain vested interests, most obviously the hotel industry who stand to lose out from the new competition from Airbnb.
  Mark Fenwick, Wulf A. Kaal, Erik P. M. Vermeulen, Regulation Tomorrow What Happens When Technology Is Faster Than the Law (2017). SSRN: https://ssrn.com/abstract=2834531
- [2834531-024](https://wulfkaal.github.io/claims/2834531-024) [failure/argued] *(failure mode)* -- The premises on which regulation of a new technology is built may not be facts at all, or at least not the most relevant facts about that innovation, as the assumption that Airbnb hosts are simply chasing easy money illustrates.
  > The selected facts may not even be facts, or at least, the most relevant facts about a particular innovation.
  Mark Fenwick, Wulf A. Kaal, Erik P. M. Vermeulen, Regulation Tomorrow What Happens When Technology Is Faster Than the Law (2017). SSRN: https://ssrn.com/abstract=2834531
- [2834531-026](https://wulfkaal.github.io/claims/2834531-026) [failure/argued] *(failure mode)* -- There is a systematic disconnect between the facts regulators identify as important and the actual experience and wishes of most consumers.
  > There is a disconnect between the facts that regulators identify as important and the experience and wishes of most consumers.
  Mark Fenwick, Wulf A. Kaal, Erik P. M. Vermeulen, Regulation Tomorrow What Happens When Technology Is Faster Than the Law (2017). SSRN: https://ssrn.com/abstract=2834531
- [2834531-027](https://wulfkaal.github.io/claims/2834531-027) [failure/argued] *(failure mode)* -- The problem is not regulator bad faith: the acceleration of innovation cycles means that selecting the relevant facts is difficult even under the best conditions and with the best intentions, while simply waiting is likely to bring further complications and criticism.

  Mark Fenwick, Wulf A. Kaal, Erik P. M. Vermeulen, Regulation Tomorrow What Happens When Technology Is Faster Than the Law (2017). SSRN: https://ssrn.com/abstract=2834531
