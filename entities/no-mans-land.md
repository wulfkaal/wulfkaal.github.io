- [2029983-015](https://wulfkaal.github.io/claims/2029983-015) [failure/argued] *(failure mode)* -- Geographic tests create the risk of a no man's land transaction: defendants may persuade the courts of every jurisdiction that the transaction took place outside their borders, leaving the transaction governed by no law and with no available forum.
  > Alternatively, defendants might successfully persuade courts in all jurisdictions that a transaction took place outside their borders or for some other reason outside their jurisdictions. Such a "no man's land" transaction would be governed by no law and there would be no forum.
  Wulf A. Kaal, Richard W. Painter, Forum Competition and Choice of Law Competition in Securities Law after Morrison v. National Austral (2012). SSRN: https://ssrn.com/abstract=2029983
- [2029983-037](https://wulfkaal.github.io/claims/2029983-037) [failure/argued] *(failure mode)* -- A contract selecting non-U.S. securities law can fail entirely: if the chosen jurisdiction's courts decline jurisdiction because the transaction did not clear there or the parties lack a local presence, the contract may as a practical matter mean that no law applies.
  > If so, the contract to apply non- U.S. law might as a practical matter mean that no law applies.
  Wulf A. Kaal, Richard W. Painter, Forum Competition and Choice of Law Competition in Securities Law after Morrison v. National Austral (2012). SSRN: https://ssrn.com/abstract=2029983
